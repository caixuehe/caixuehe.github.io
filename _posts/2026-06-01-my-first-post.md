## 论文背景

Modern companies receive resumes in volumes that overwhelm Human Resources (HR) staff during the screening stage. Large Language Model (LLM)–based screening tools have emerged as a practical way to identify suitable candidates before the interview. However, the validity of such systems — in particular, the *stability* of the scores they produce — has rarely been tested. Rhea et al. (2022) develop a *socio-technical* audit framework for interrogating the validity of one specific class of algorithmic pre-hiring assessments: personality-scoring systems built on resumes and social profiles. Applied to two well-known commercial products (**Humantic AI** and **Crystal**), the audit shows that output scores vary substantially with the *format* of the input, even when the underlying information is identical.

📄 Paper: [An External Stability Audit Framework to Test the Validity of Personality Prediction in AI Hiring](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.09151) (Rhea et al., *Data Mining and Knowledge Discovery*, 2022)
